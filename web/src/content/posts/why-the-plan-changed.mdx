The first plan was to pretrain a 50 to 150 million parameter model and grow it, over 64 experiments, into a local coding and writing assistant. Day 3 had four experiments built and ready for a GPU. And right before the first run I stopped and asked, okay, is this actually the right track ? Working through our own numbers said no, it wasn't.

<Label>Fact 1</Label>

## The Day 2 models were starved of data

Hoffmann et al. found that for a fixed compute budget, the best loss comes from training on about 20 tokens per parameter. Fewer tokens means the model is bigger than the budget can use.

The Day 2 grid trained models of about 3.3 million parameters for 400 steps of 8 blocks of 256 tokens, so 819,200 tokens each. That's about 0.25 tokens per parameter, 1 percent of the compute-optimal budget. The Day 3 plan raised the steps fivefold, to 4.1 million tokens, which is still only about 1.1 tokens per parameter. Its long-context config reached 8.2 million tokens, and the whole Day 2 corpus holds 2.96 million, so it would have repeated data too.

And that explains [the Day 2 grid](/posts/reading-the-grid/). A seed spread of 0.043 bits per byte, bigger than most of the effects we were measuring, is just what an undertrained model looks like. Where it ends up depends a lot on where it started. Stage 0 would have measured that spread again, at a budget that's still about 18 times too small.

### So what happened ?

- The Day 2 grid is at about 1 percent of its optimal token budget. The Day 3 plan is at about 5 percent.
- The Day 4 main run, 26 million parameters on 393 million tokens, gets to about 15 tokens per parameter. That's close enough to actually read results.
- Switch the throughput between the planned 15 TFLOPs and the 4.1 that EXP-066 measured on a real T4. At the measured rate the Day 4 run takes about 4 hours, a 50M model at 1 billion tokens takes about 20, and a 150M model is just out of reach.

That last row is why pretraining stops around 20 million parameters.

<Label>Fact 2</Label>

## The goal needs a model that follows instructions

My goal with this is to learn ML properly, like in depth, for placement season, and to end up with a harness that can control my own model. A harness shows the model a task and some tools, parses each tool call it emits, runs the tool and feeds back the result. That needs a model that follows instructions and produces well-formed tool calls. No model you can pretrain on free Colab does either of those.

<Label>The decision</Label>

## Keep the from-scratch model, then move the same code to Qwen

So the new plan has two halves:

1. **Train the from-scratch model once, properly.** The resulting model has 26.3 million parameters, on TinyStories, with the Day 2 `modern` stack. TinyStories is a dataset where models under about 30 million parameters write coherent English, and the run fits the measured compute. Day 4 trained it, and [Day 5 compares and speeds it up](/posts/seed-spread-at-scale/).
2. **Load Qwen into the same model code.** `octlm/model.py` already has RoPE, RMSNorm, SwiGLU and grouped-query attention, the same parts Qwen uses. Match its logits, write LoRA, fine-tune it for tool calls, and build the harness and its eval around it. That's Day 6 to Day 9.

Two alternatives got rejected. Continuing the first plan spends the remaining time on variants the harness never uses, and ends with a model too weak to drive it. Fine-tuning Qwen with an off-the-shelf trainer gives the harness a working model, but then there's nothing to explain when an interviewer asks how attention, the KV cache or LoRA actually work. And that's kind of the whole point.

<Label>What was dropped</Label>

## Where each first-plan experiment went

Every first-plan experiment got a new number, a merge into another experiment, or a reason it was dropped. New numbers start at EXP-065, so no number ever means two things. The table is read from `notes/day3.md` when the site builds.

The dropped groups share reasons:

- **MoE, MLA, sparse and compressed attention, Muon, mHC and the DeepSeek-style runs** don't help the harness. The built Day 3 code stays behind default-off flags.
- **DDP, FSDP, tensor and pipeline parallelism** need more than one GPU, and the free tiers give you one.
- **Pretraining at 50 to 150 million parameters, and the scaling check,** are ruled out by the compute table above.
- **DPO and the reward model.** Supervised fine-tuning on harness traces is the post-training the eval can actually measure.
- **Retrieval, the writing assistant, the cache stack, batching and speculative decoding.** The harness uses `grep` for retrieval and serves one request at a time.

<Label>The rules</Label>

## Conflicts the change created, and how they were recorded

`AGENTS.md` says don't start a day before every exit check of the previous day passes. None of Day 3's passed. But the user's request outranks the plan, so the Day 3 checks were recorded as withdrawn, not failed, and Day 4 starts from Day 2's passed checks.

`AGENTS.md` also says every tokenizer learns its merges from our own data. Qwen's tokenizer ships with its weights. So the rule keeps applying to every tokenizer octlm trains, and for Qwen the checkpoint loader applies the hash check to `tokenizer.json` instead.
