[A language model predicts](/posts/next-token-prediction/) a probability for every token in the vocabulary. Generation turns that into text one token at a time. It picks a token, appends it, and runs the model again. Up to Day 3, octlm always picked the most likely token. Day 4 added sampling, because the exit check judges the model's stories, and greedy decoding would make it judge the decoding instead.

<Label>The problem</Label>

## Greedy decoding loops

Greedy decoding takes the argmax at every step. It is deterministic, and on story data it repeats itself. Once a phrase like "and they were very happy" becomes the most likely continuation of itself, the model says it again, and again. Sampling breaks the loop by sometimes picking a less likely token, and two settings control how often and how far.

<Label>Temperature</Label>

## Divide the scores before the softmax

Temperature $\tau$ divides every logit before the softmax:

$$
p_i = \frac{e^{z_i / \tau}}{\sum_j e^{z_j / \tau}}
$$

At $\tau = 1$ the distribution is the model's own. Below 1 the gaps between scores grow, and the distribution sharpens toward the top token. Above 1 the gaps shrink, and it flattens toward uniform. As $\tau$ approaches 0 it becomes greedy, which is why octlm treats temperature 0 as argmax rather than dividing by zero.

<Label>Top-k</Label>

## Keep only the k best tokens

Top-k sets every logit outside the $k$ largest to $-\infty$ before the softmax, so those tokens get exactly zero probability. Temperature alone never removes a token. With thousands of unlikely tokens, their combined probability can be large enough that the model regularly picks nonsense. Top-k cuts that tail.

### What happened?

- At temperature 0, every draw is `" happy"`. That is greedy decoding.
- At 0.5 the top token takes most of the probability. At 1.5 the less likely tokens get real shares, and the entropy rises toward the 3 bits of a uniform choice among eight.
- Top-k 3 zeroes five tokens outright, whatever the temperature. The remaining three renormalize to sum to 1.
- The draw counts follow the probabilities but not exactly. With 200 draws, a token at p = 0.1 lands anywhere from about 12 to 28 times.

`Decoder.generate` gained `temperature`, `top_k` and a `torch.Generator`. The default temperature is 0, so every earlier day's greedy output is unchanged.

<Label>Skipped</Label>

## What we did not build, and why

- **Top-p, or nucleus, sampling.** It keeps the smallest set of tokens whose probabilities sum to p, which adapts to how confident the model is. Top-k with temperature is enough to stop greedy loops, and adding top-p is one line if the samples need it.
- **Repetition penalties.** They hide a weak model's repetition instead of measuring it.
- **Beam search.** It searches for the single most likely continuation, which makes repetition worse on open-ended text.
