Day 2 ran every component comparison as one grid on a Colab T4. Each variant changes one thing about the Day 1 baseline, except `modern`, which combines the four keepers. Every variant trained with three different random seeds at the same budget. This post reads the whole grid, and honestly the lesson here is about how to read it, not about any one component.

<Label>The setup</Label>

## What was held fixed

- Width 256, 4 layers, 8 query heads and a 256-token context, about 3.3 million parameters.
- 400 steps at batch 8, so 819,200 training tokens per run.
- The same corpus, tokenizer and validation blocks for every run.
- Seeds 1337, 1338 and 1339, the same three for every variant.
- The grid's 24 runs took 249 seconds of training and evaluation on the T4, about 11 times faster per step than the laptop.

The grid reports bits per byte separately on held-out code and prose, the mean over three seeds, and the spread, which is the largest seed's result minus the smallest.

<Label>The noise floor</Label>

## Read the spread before the gap

The baseline, with nothing changed except the seed, moved {row("baseline").codeSpread} bits per byte on code across its three runs. The seed changes the initial weights and the order of the batches, and at 400 steps that's enough to move the result by 0.04. So any difference between two variants smaller than that could just be the seeds.

Read that way, the grid says:

| Variant | Change | Code gap vs baseline | Verdict |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| rmsnorm | [RMSNorm](/blogs/rmsnorm/) | {(row("rmsnorm").code - row("baseline").code).toFixed(4)} | Inside the noise |
| swiglu | [SwiGLU](/blogs/swiglu/) | {(row("swiglu").code - row("baseline").code).toFixed(4)} | Outside the noise, keep |
| post-norm | [Post-norm](/blogs/residual-placement/) | {(row("post-norm").code - row("baseline").code).toFixed(4)} | Far outside, revert |
| gqa-4 | [4 KV heads](/blogs/gqa-and-kv-cache/) | {(row("gqa-4").code - row("baseline").code).toFixed(4)} | Inside the noise |
| gqa-2 | 2 KV heads | {(row("gqa-2").code - row("baseline").code).toFixed(4)} | Inside the noise, keep for the cache |
| mqa-1 | 1 KV head | {(row("mqa-1").code - row("baseline").code).toFixed(4)} | Inside the noise |
| modern | RoPE, RMSNorm, SwiGLU, 2 KV heads | {(row("modern").code - row("baseline").code).toFixed(4)} | Best mean, widest spread |

Negative is better. Two of six single changes gave a readable result, one good and one bad. The rest can't be told apart from the baseline at this budget. And "can't be told apart" is not "equal", a real 0.01 improvement would look exactly like this.

<Label>The open question</Label>

## `modern` against `swiglu`, unresolved

The combined stack has the best code and prose means in the grid, with a quarter of the baseline's cache. Its margin over `swiglu` alone is {(row("swiglu").code - row("modern").code).toFixed(3)} on code. But its own seed spread is {row("modern").codeSpread}, almost three times the baseline's. So the grid ranks the two and can't separate them.

The note's diagnosis is that the fix is more steps, not more seeds. More seeds would give a better estimate of a noisy number. More training would make the number less noisy in the first place, because an undertrained model's result depends a lot on where it started. At 3.27 million parameters and 2.96 million training tokens, each model saw about one token per parameter. Compute-optimal training wants about 20.

<Label>What it led to</Label>

## The grid is why the plan changed

Day 3 planned to fix this by raising the budget fivefold and remeasuring the noise first, as "Stage 0". But when I worked out the numbers, even that budget would be about 18 times too small, and the corpus would repeat. [Why the plan changed](/blogs/why-the-plan-changed/) covers the arithmetic. [Day 5 measured the seed spread again](/blogs/seed-spread-at-scale/) at 98.3M tokens per run. The larger range fell to 0.0050 bits per byte, and the modern stack beat the baseline by 0.0338, well clear of it.

The rule that survives is that every comparison states its noise floor first, and a gap inside it gets recorded as unresolved with its numbers, not as a win.
